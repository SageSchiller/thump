# Thump

A 16-pad drum machine and beat maker for the m-vave SMC-PAD Pocket, running in a Chromium
app window. Works with the pad over USB or Bluetooth (both show up as normal MIDI ports on
Linux), plus mouse and keyboard.

It looks and works like a piece of hardware: a graphite body with silkscreened labels, a
glowing display (tempo, pattern, bar, beat and step, and a live spectrum of what you hear that sets its own sensitivity like a recording meter, from
kick drums on the left to hi-hats on the right),
pads lit from inside in each sound's color that flare when hit, knobs you drag up and down,
keys with indicator lights, and a step grid that lights up as the beat runs.

## Run

    thump            # or "Thump" in the app launcher

The launcher (`./thump`) opens Chromium with its own profile in `~/.local/share/thump/browser`,
lets sound start without a click, stops Chromium slowing the app down when its window is in the
background, turns on Web Bluetooth (for the pad lights without a cable), and asks Bluetooth to
connect the pad if it is switched on. Chromium asks once to
let Thump control MIDI devices (needed for the pad lights) and the microphone the first time you
record: choose Allow.

## What it does

- **318 sounds**: 86 original synth sounds (dubstep, riddim and neuro basses, industrial metal
  and noise, builds, risers, drops, pads) made in `synth.js`, and 232 drum samples from 12
  freely licensed Hydrogen kits (see CREDITS.md, generated by the build).
- **13 ready-made kits** (Dubstep, Riddim / neuro, Audiophob dubstep, Industrial, Factory,
  Electro, Trap, Drum machines, Hand drums, Heavy metal, Rock, Hip-hop, Classic 808).
- **Your own kits**: change the sounds and settings on the pads, then save them as a kit
  (Kits in the top bar). Your kits sit next to the ready-made ones in the Kit list.
- **Four presets, like the pad's**: the SMC-PAD Pocket has four presets, picked by holding pad
  13, 14, 15 or 16 while you switch it on. Each holds a kit, and Thump follows the pad's preset.
  To switch while you play, let pads 13 to 16 switch presets (one button in Kits), or click the
  keys above the pads. Put any kit on any preset in the Kits panel, or
  fill all four at once with a set of ready-made kits (Bass music, Industrial, Old school, Live
  drums) or with your own. Each preset keeps its pads as you left them. Following the pad needs
  the same link to the pad as the lights (USB, or Bluetooth once connected in Lights).
- **Pad controls**: any pad can work a control instead of playing its sound (right-click it and
  pick one from the menu, or hit it and use Pad does in the panel): note repeat while held, repeat on/off and rate, latch (held sounds and
  repeats keep going until you hit the pad again), stop all sound, presets 1 to 4 or next and
  previous, next and previous pattern, swing, tap tempo, metronome, play/stop, record, undo,
  and mute while held. Controls stay on their pads when you switch kits. On the pad, control
  pads glow orange, brighter while their control is on.
- **Per pad**: sound, volume, pitch, length, drive, pan, hold, reverse, cut group. Turn a knob
  by dragging up or down (Shift for fine moves) or scrolling over it; double-click resets it.
- **Patterns and songs**: 8 patterns (A to H) of 1, 2 or 4 bars. Click a pattern while playing
  and it switches at the end of the pass. Chain patterns into a song (A ×2, B ×4, ...).
- **Recording**: count-in, metronome, snap, undo per pass, note repeat, click-to-edit grid.
- **Groove and mix**: swing, sidechain pump (on kicks or every beat), master filter and reverb,
  mute rows (or "Mute with pads" to drop parts out live).
- **Notes mode**: one sound on all 16 pads as a scale, for basslines.
- **Wobble designer**: speed (with a different speed for the second half of the bar), shape,
  wave, filter range, resonance, growl, grit, sub and width. Designs follow the tempo.
- **Your own sounds**: drop audio files on a pad, record with the microphone (silence is
  trimmed), or chop a long sound across the pads (by detected hits or 16 equal slices).
- **Pad lights** (SMC-PAD Pocket, over USB or Bluetooth), set in the Lights panel: each pad in its
  sound's color, in colors you pick (click a pad on screen or hit it on the pad), or in the pad's
  own colors; brightness; flashes when you hit and when the beat plays, brighter or white; dark when
  muted or empty. Hits are read through the pad's own note layout, so every preset lands on the
  right pad, and the lights follow preset and bank changes. Thump never saves to the pad: its own
  colors come back when Thump closes, when the lights are turned off, or when the pad is switched
  off.
- **Save audio**: render a pattern (×1 to ×8) or the whole song to MP3 or WAV, faster than
  real time. Beats save in the app and export to `.thump.json` (with your own sounds inside).

Esc stops all sound. Space plays and stops. Ctrl+Z undoes.

## Pad layout

Pad 1 is bottom left (note 36), counting left to right, bottom row up to pad 16 top right
(note 51). Pads send on MIDI channel 10. Any note is folded onto the 16 pads, so pad banks
still work.

## Files

- `index.html`, `style.css`, `app.js`: the app
- `synth.js`: synth sound library and wobble designer (rendered offline with Web Audio)
- `presets.js`: the ready-made kits
- `kits.js`: generated sample library (base64 Ogg Opus), built by `build_kits.py`
- `sources/`: Hydrogen kit archives used by the build (Hydrogen itself is not needed)
- `vendor/lamejs.js`: MP3 encoder (LGPL, `vendor/lamejs-LICENSE.txt`)
- `fonts/`: Barlow Condensed, Big Shoulders Stencil, Share Tech Mono and DSEG7 Classic, with
  their licenses
- `tools/cdp.mjs`: headless Chromium test driver

## Build and test

    python3 build_kits.py      # rebuild kits.js and CREDITS.md (needs ffmpeg)
    node tools/cdp.mjs "file://$PWD/index.html?selftest" --wait 1000 --eval \
      "new Promise(r => { const t = setInterval(() => { if (document.title.startsWith('SELFTEST')) { clearInterval(t); r(document.getElementById('selftest').textContent); } }, 500); })"

`build_kits.py` also builds optional extra kits into `kits-extra.js` from archives in
`sources/extra/`, for kits from Hydrogen's library that don't state a license. Neither is in
this repository; the app loads `kits-extra.js` if it is there.

The self-test (215 checks) checks every preset sound exists, renders every synth sound and 15
wobble designs, decodes every sample, and checks loop timing, swing, song order, pattern
switching, old beat files, offline rendering, WAV and MP3 encoding, mic trimming, the
chopper's hit detection, your kits and the four presets, pad controls, the spectrum's levels and auto gain, and the pad
lights and preset following against a pretend pad. `--fake-media` gives headless Chromium a fake microphone; `--shot
file.png` saves a screenshot.

## How the pad lights work

The pad ignores MIDI notes sent to it. Its colors live in the settings memory that M-VAVE's
editor app changes with SysEx on the USB "Private" port, using a protocol reverse-engineered by
[spectalive/smc-pad](https://github.com/spectalive/smc-pad) for the bigger SMC-PAD; the Pocket
uses the same protocol. Its memory holds 4 presets of 2931 bytes, each 112 pad records (7 banks
of 16) of 26 bytes, with the pad's red, green and blue at bytes 5 to 7. The pad only takes writes
after a full read of its settings, kept alive by reading its 12-byte global block about three
times a second. Byte 10 of that block is the preset the pad is on (found by
[rjpenny16/smc-pad-studio](https://github.com/rjpenny16/smc-pad-studio)), which is how Thump
follows the pad's presets. When the pad moves to another preset or bank, the pads Thump lit
there get their own colors back.
Over Bluetooth the same packets go raw (no SysEx) to GATT characteristic `AE41` of service
`AE40`, with answers on `AE42`. Thump reaches it with Web Bluetooth, which Chromium on Linux only
offers with `--enable-features=WebBluetooth,WebBluetoothNewPermissionsBackend` (the launcher passes
it). The first time, Connect over Bluetooth in the Lights panel opens Chromium's device chooser;
after that Thump reconnects by itself. With the cable in, Thump uses USB. Chromium on Linux
sometimes hands over each notification twice at once; Thump drops those repeats. When Thump
closes, it puts the pad's own colors back over either link.
